Round 2 — subtle · clean · dual-theme #13–#22 · every concept ships light + dark with a live toggle

Built from your feedback: restrained palettes, both themes designed first-class, and each concept organized around a focus mechanism — a distinct answer to "how does the eye instantly know what matters?"

#13 · ONE SATURATED ELEMENT Halo Bento's hue-per-section idea, whispered — neutral chrome with 5% section tints, and the only saturated thing on screen is the action you should take. #14 · CONTRAST STAGING Stage Light follows selection — the active region runs at full contrast while everything else steps back a level, like stage lighting. #15 · COLOR = OBLIGATION Graphite A masterfully tuned grayscale interface where copper appears only where your action is required — zero false alarms, zero decoration. #16 · ATTENTION-FIRST Tempo Every screen leads with a slim, pre-triaged "Needs you" strip — you never scan for what's pending, the UI already ranked it. #17 · EDITORIAL HIERARCHY Vellum Quiet-luxury print craft — warm neutrals in both themes, serif display for what matters, one copper call-to-action per screen. #18 · SHARPNESS = FOCUS Lens Depth of field — the thing that matters is perfectly crisp while the rest sits a breath back, pulling focus as you move. #19 · AMBIENT TEMPERATURE Tide A neutral room whose edges warm with the agent's state — cool when idle, amber while working, copper when something waits on you. #20 · CENTER = FOCUS Meridian Geometry does the work — the active thing always lives in a centered column with luxurious margins; everything else is periphery. #21 · ELEVATION STEPS Terrace Exactly three surface levels, rigorously enforced — the one thing needing you physically sits on the top terrace in both themes. #22 · FOLLOW THE THREAD Prism The quietest possible shell plus one continuous gradient thread running from where you are to what to do next — the room's only color.

Round 1 — twelve visual worlds #01–#12 · single-theme statement pieces

The first exploration: maximum spread across dark/light, dense/sparse, serious/expressive. Kept here for reference and for stealing details from.

#01 · DARK / LUXURY Aurora Deep-space glassmorphism — frosted panels floating over a slowly drifting aurora; the agent's presence breathes as a halo of light around the input. #02 · LIGHT / EDITORIAL Ledger A broadsheet financial paper — warm stock, serif headlines, hairline rules; chat as printed correspondence and approvals as inked stamps. #03 · DARK / DENSE Ops Deck A modern financial terminal — all-mono phosphor green on black, keyboard hints everywhere, a live status line, and a chat that reads like a session log. #04 · LIGHT / INSTRUMENT Rams "Less, but better" — a Braun instrument panel in warm gray with one signal-orange accent, physical toggles, and a dial gauge for the headline stat. #05 · DARK / SCI-FI Vector HUD A cockpit hologram — cyan linework, clipped corners and corner brackets, telemetry-style tool readouts, and a reticle that locks onto your selection. #06 · DARK / ORGANIC Terra Technology that feels grown — moss and clay tones, blob-radius panels, and a breathing gradient orb as the agent's living presence. #07 · LIGHT / BRUTALIST Concrete Unapologetic brutalism — black 2px borders, zero radius, massive type, one acid-chartreuse highlight, and a mantra marquee along the floor. #08 · LIGHT / ZEN Sumi Ink on washi — radical whitespace, vertical scroll-label nav, a vermilion seal for the one action that matters, and an ensō that draws itself while the agent thinks. #09 · BLUE / DRAFTING Blueprint A cyanotype drafting sheet — pure white linework on blueprint blue, callout tabs and leader lines, an APPROVED stamp, and a real title block at the foot. #10 · DARK / TYPOGRAPHIC Signal Swiss modernism with no boxes at all — structure from type, rules and a strict grid, giant numerals, and exactly one red element per screen. #11 · LIGHT / PLAYFUL Bento A bento-grid OS — every section owns a hue, cards feel like trays with springy press states, and the whole chrome re-tints as you move around. #12 · DARK / AMBIENT Ember Calm computing on OLED black — the agent is a softly glowing ember orb whose light is the status system; approvals smolder until you decide.
